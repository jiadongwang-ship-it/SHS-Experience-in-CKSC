      <!-- Introduction -->
      <h2>1. What is a Network?</h2>
      <p class="first-line-indent">
        A network is a collection of technologies that allow computers to connect and communicate with one another. 
        Through networks, devices can share files, data, resources, and internet access.
      </p>

      <h2>2. What is the Internet?</h2>
      <p class="first-line-indent">
        The Internet is communication between different networks worldwide. 
        It is a global system of interconnected computer networks using standard protocols, making information accessible everywhere.
      </p>

      <h2>3. What is Ethernet?</h2>
      <p class="first-line-indent">
        Ethernet is a way of connecting computers within a Local Area Network (LAN). 
        It is commonly used in homes, schools, and offices to enable communication between devices.
      </p>

      <!-- COMPONENTS -->
      <h2>4. Components of Ethernet</h2>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>UTP Cable (Unshielded Twisted Pair)</strong> – Copper cable composed of twisted wire pairs.</li>
        <li><strong>CAT5</strong> – Supports up to 100 MHz and speeds up to 2.5 Gbps.</li>
        <li><strong>CAT6</strong> – Supports 250 MHz and speeds up to 10 Gbps.</li>
        <li><strong>RJ45 Connector</strong> – Standard connector used for Ethernet cable ends.</li>
        <li><strong>Ethernet Port</strong> – The socket on computers, routers, or switches where Ethernet cables are plugged.</li>
      </ul>

      <h2>5. Tools Used in Ethernet Setup</h2>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Stripping Tool</strong> – Removes cable jackets.</li>
        <li><strong>Crimping Tool</strong> – Attaches RJ45 connectors onto cables.</li>
        <li><strong>LAN Tester</strong> – Checks if the Ethernet cable is working properly.</li>
        <li><strong>Scissors</strong> – Used for cutting cable materials.</li>
      </ul>

      <h2>6. Types of Ethernet Cables</h2>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Cross-over Cable</strong> – Connects two computers directly without a router.</li>
        <li><strong>Straight-through Cable</strong> – Connects a computer to a router or switch.</li>
      </ul>

      <!-- ETHERNET STEPS -->
      <h2>7. Steps in Making an Ethernet Cable</h2>
      <ul>
        <li>Cut the cable to the needed length.</li>
        <li>Strip off around 1 inch of the outer jacket.</li>
        <li>Untwist and arrange the wires in the correct order.</li>
        <li>Trim wires evenly.</li>
        <li>Insert the wires into the RJ45 plug.</li>
        <li>Crimp using the crimping tool.</li>
        <li>Test with a LAN tester.</li>
      </ul>

      <!-- FIBER OPTIC -->
      <h2>8. Fiber Optic Cable</h2>
      <p class="first-line-indent">
        A fiber optic cable contains glass fibers inside a protective casing. 
        It supports high-performance networking with extremely fast speeds and long-distance connections.
      </p>

      <h2>9. Components of Fiber Optic Setup</h2>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>SC Connector</strong> – A push-pull connector for fiber cables.</li>
        <li><strong>Media Converter</strong> – Converts Ethernet signals to fiber signals.</li>
      </ul>

      <h2>10. Tools for Fiber Optic Setup</h2>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Fiber Stripper</strong> – Removes fiber jacket.</li>
        <li><strong>Drop Cable Stripper</strong> – Strips outer fiber casing.</li>
        <li><strong>Fiber Cleaver</strong> – Cuts fiber precisely.</li>
        <li><strong>Fault Locator</strong> – Detects breaks or faults in the fiber.</li>
      </ul>

      <h2>11. Steps in Fiber Cable Setup</h2>
      <ul>
        <li>Cut the cable to the needed length.</li>
        <li>Strip the outer jacket.</li>
        <li>Strip the inner jelly jacket.</li>
        <li>Cleave the fiber thread using the fiber cleaver.</li>
        <li>Insert the fiber into the SC connector.</li>
        <li>Secure the connector.</li>
        <li>Test the cable using a fault locator.</li>
      </ul>

      <h2>12. Significance of the Topic</h2>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Security</strong> – Networks enable secure data transfer.</li>
        <li><strong>Life Skills</strong> – Learning networking prepares you for real-world tech use.</li>
        <li><strong>Business</strong> – Companies rely heavily on networking and communication systems.</li>
      </ul>
